Intel for GC AI: Member of Technical Staff, Product Engineering Seat

This is the longer read on the Product Engineering seat at GC AI: what the company is, how it got here, what the work looks like, and how hiring works.

What GC AI is

Legal AI for in-house legal teams. It is the software a company's own lawyers use to draft, review and analyze contracts and other legal documents in seconds, with the accuracy a lawyer needs before signing off.

GC stands for general counsel. That is the customer. More than 2,200 in-house legal teams use it every day, including News Corp, Vercel, Webflow, Nextdoor, Skims, Liquid Death, Zscaler and TIME.

What it does today:

  • Answers legal questions and reviews documents in chat.
  • Redlines contracts directly inside Microsoft Word.
  • Follows each team's own negotiation playbook, so it pushes back the way they would.

The mission

Most legal AI is built for law firms or for everyone. GC AI builds for the lawyers inside a company, and the next step is agents that help those teams serve the business faster.

"That's why we built something lawyers actually use and trust, and our 70 NPS proves that." Cecilia Ziniti, co-founder and CEO

How it started

In early 2022, before ChatGPT, Cecilia Ziniti was testing language models on contract drafting and review.

She teamed up with Bardia Pourvakil, an engineer she worked with at Replit. They started with a newsletter and by teaching lawyers how to prompt. Those early students showed them who needed the product most: in-house teams. The company started in 2023 and the product launched in April 2024.

The founders and the team

  • Cecilia Ziniti, co-founder and CEO. Berkeley Law. Founding lawyer on Amazon Alexa, lead counsel at Cruise, then general counsel three times, the last at Replit. Four patents. She is building for the job she did.
  • Bardia Pourvakil, co-founder and CTO. Self-taught. Built the API at Roam Research and shipped GPT-3 inside a product in 2020, then joined Replit.
  • The engineering team is under twenty people and doubling this year. Every engineer holds the same title, Member of Technical Staff.
  • About one in five people at the company is a lawyer. They work directly with engineering, and some submit code that engineers review and ship.

Why they are hiring now

The product is growing faster than the team that builds it. Thousands of lawyers work inside it every day.

So GC AI is adding six product engineers, the largest group they are hiring. It is the team that builds what lawyers see and touch.

The role

A full-stack product seat. You own a feature from the API to the screen, and you work directly with the founding team.

What you would do

  • Design and build full-stack features, from the back end to the interface.
  • Own the surfaces lawyers use every day to review, draft and advise.
  • Make the architecture calls across React, TypeScript and Node.js, on Google Cloud.
  • Turn customer feedback into a spec, then ship it quickly.
  • Raise the bar on reliability with testing, monitoring and fallbacks, so AI features behave the same way every time.
  • Help other engineers through code review.

What they ask for

  • Six or more years building production web applications, at a senior or staff level.
  • Strong modern TypeScript and deep React experience.
  • Real back end work: systems you designed that scaled, on a cloud platform.
  • Architecture decisions of yours that held up over time.
  • A career spent at product companies, with real ownership at startup size.

Who would be successful

  • You like owning a whole feature, front and back, and you are doing product-facing work right now.
  • You have been a founder or a founding engineer, or you moved from a large company to a small one and took on more there.
  • You have stayed long enough in your jobs to see what you built grow up.
  • You care how the product feels to the person using it, and you care that it works every time.
  • You build with AI tools because you enjoy it. Experience with AI features is a plus, not a must.

The tech stack

  • TypeScript and JavaScript
  • React
  • Node.js
  • Google Cloud
  • Modern ORMs such as Drizzle and Prisma
  • AI coding tools, including Claude Code, as part of the daily work

The interview process

  • A recruiter call, about 30 minutes.
  • An engineering deep dive on your past work, about 60 minutes.
  • A technical interview.
  • A virtual onsite in three parts: plan, about 45 minutes. Build, about two hours. Review, about 45 minutes.
  • A values conversation with people from across the company, about 30 minutes.
  • A conversation with leadership, about 30 minutes.
