The problem, in one picture

Nobody swaps Chase dollars for Bank of America dollars before paying rent. Stablecoins aren't there yet. USDC, PYUSD and dozens more are all digital dollars, but moving between them means trading, slippage and waiting. Like holding gift cards from ten stores when you just want cash.

TBMC is the clearinghouse that makes every compliant digital dollar worth exactly one dollar, everywhere. Swaps are 1:1, the fee is fixed, settlement time is guaranteed. A fintech or bank plugs in once and reaches the whole ecosystem.

Why them, and not someone else

Three other ways exist to move between stablecoins today. Each has a built-in limit TBMC doesn't.

Issuer networks

Circle, Stripe and Paxos each run networks built around their own coin. A rival issuer won't route its money through a competitor's pipes.

Trading venues

Exchanges and OTC desks treat dollars like a trading asset. Prices move, fees vary, and finance teams can't predict what lands.

Build it yourself

Every fintech wires up issuer by issuer, chain by chain. Slow, expensive and never finished.

TBMC

Neutral. Not an issuer, so it doesn't pick a winner. Rival coins clear on the same rail at par, with one integration.

The moat

  • Clearing is a network game. Like Visa or ACH, the layer everyone uses becomes the default. TBMC already got competing issuers onto one rail, the hardest part: Paxos, Bridge (Stripe), MoonPay, Agora, M0, Frax.
  • Demand is lined up too: Ramp, Modern Treasury, BitGo, LayerZero, MetaMask, Phantom.
  • Built for banks to say yes. Its own licensed payments entity, SOC 2 Type 2, and only GENIUS Act compliant coins.
  • Backed by the people who built the coins. Sean Neville, co-founder of Circle (the company behind USDC), invested.

Why now

The GENIUS Act gave U.S. stablecoins a rulebook. Stripe bought Bridge, Mastercard agreed to buy BVNK for up to $1.8B, and Klarna, Sony and Fiserv are launching dollar tokens. Every new coin adds fragmentation and needs a neutral place to clear.
