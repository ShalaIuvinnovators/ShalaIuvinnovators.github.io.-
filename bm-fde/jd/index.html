Intel for The Better Money Company: Forward Deployed Engineer Seat

This is the longer read on the Forward Deployed Engineer seat at The Better Money Company: what the company is, how it got here, what the work looks like, and how hiring works. Take what is useful.

What The Better Money Company is

A clearinghouse for stablecoins. Today every issuer's digital dollar is a different token, and moving between them costs time and money. Better Money lets banks, fintechs and payment companies send one stablecoin in and get another out, one for one, with a fixed fee and a guaranteed settlement time. Their own line: $1 in, $1 out.

It already supports 8 of the top 10 issuance platforms and moves real money for customers today.

The mission

Make stablecoins work like money, not like a trading asset.

"I'm building The Better Money Company because I am offended by the status quo and impatient for the future. The fundamental system of value must be faster, more open, more flexible, and more resilient, but we will have to make it so." Sam Broner, founder

How it started

Sam Broner spent more than two years as the stablecoin investor at a16z crypto. New digital dollars kept launching, and there was no one place that connected them. In the 1800s, clearinghouses did that job for banks. In November 2025 he left to build one for stablecoins with Adam Zuckerman, a friend from college.

They raised $10M in March 2026 and were moving customer money within six months of starting.

Why they are hiring now

The GENIUS Act made stablecoins regulated U.S. money, and companies like Klarna, Cloudflare, Sony and Fiserv are launching their own. Each new coin is one more thing to connect, and each new customer needs to be plugged in. That work is this seat.

The role

You sit between the company and its customers, mostly banks and payment companies. You come in earlier than a typical forward deployed engineer: you help decide what gets built with a partner, and then you build it. This is an engineer who works with customers, not a salesperson who codes.

What you would do

  • Own each customer integration from the first scoping call to go-live.
  • Turn what customers ask for into what the product team builds next.
  • Build the shared tools, SDKs and documentation that make the next integration faster.
  • Be the first responder when something breaks between the company and a partner.
  • Talk to users and write the code yourself.

What they ask for

  • Three years or more shipping integrations with payment systems, financial infrastructure or banking partners.
  • You have been on the other end of the API and know what a good integration feels like.
  • You can explain engineering to a customer and a customer to engineering.
  • You can follow money through a complicated flow of funds.
  • Stablecoin or blockchain experience is a plus. It is not required.

Who would be successful

  • You are a strong engineer who also likes being in the room with a customer.
  • You move with urgency and treat the work as your own.
  • An open question looks like something to own, not something to wait on.
  • You have read up on the company before the first call.
  • A payment that takes three days and costs three percent bothers you.

The tech stack

TypeScript, Python, Node, PostgreSQL and cloud. ACH, wires and real-time rails on one side, stablecoin issuers and custodians on the other.

The interview process

  1. A 20 minute conversation on your background and how you work.
  2. A take-home.
  3. A paid two day work trial in the New York office: meet the team, pair on code, build something on your own, and share it back the next day.

Salary and logistics

  • Base: $200K to $225K and above.
  • Equity: yes, on a six year vest, with a larger grant for early hires.
  • Benefits: medical, dental and vision, unlimited time off, daily lunch, periodic off-sites.
  • Where: New York City, in the office five days a week, with customer and partner travel.
  • Visa: sponsorship is available.

Other open seats at The Better Money Company

  • Payments Engineer. For someone who has built payment infrastructure: ledgers, reconciliation, bank and issuer integrations.
  • Platform Engineer. For someone who builds the testing, tooling and infrastructure that keep a money system provably correct.
