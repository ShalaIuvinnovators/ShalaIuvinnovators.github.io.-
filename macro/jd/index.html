Intel for Macro: Software Engineer Seat

This is the longer read on the Software Engineer seat at Macro: what the company is, how it got here, what the work looks like, and how hiring works. Take what is useful. The code is public, so you can check most of it yourself.

What Macro is

One workspace for a whole company. Email, chat, documents, tasks, calls, sheets, a customer list and AI agents, all in one tab, and every item can link to every other one. Macro's own line is that it replaces 27 or more apps with a single system. The company runs itself on it.

The whole product is open source. More than 170,000 people use it, and it was number one on GitHub Trending in August.

The mission

Work is scattered across too many apps. Jacob Beckerman, the founder, said it this way when he announced the last funding round:

"The Office Suite should be one super app, not 11 siloed apps and chatbots." Jacob Beckerman, founder and CEO

So Macro is building the one place where a company's work lives, with AI agents that share what the whole team knows.

How it started

Jacob started Macro in New York in 2020. The first product was a smarter document reader for law firms and investment banks: it read long files and linked everything inside them. The idea kept growing. If documents should connect, so should email, chat and tasks.

In August 2026 the team shipped Macro 1.0, the full workspace, and made the entire codebase public the same day.

The founders and the team

  • Jacob Beckerman, founder and CEO. Built the company from day one and still runs it.
  • Teo Nys, co-founder and CTO. Joined as a senior engineer, grew into the role, and still writes code. He is the person you would talk to first.
  • About fifteen people, nearly all engineers, in the Flatiron office in New York, with a few engineers in Toronto.

Why it is credible

  • Backed by Andreessen Horowitz, who led both funding rounds, with BoxGroup and Craft Ventures alongside.
  • More than $30M raised.
  • 170,000+ users.
  • ISO 27001, SOC 2 and CASA Tier 2.
  • About 4,600 stars on GitHub. Anyone can read how it is built.

"Macro is the biggest change to how companies work in years." Alex Rampell, General Partner, Andreessen Horowitz

Why they are hiring now

1.0 is out and people use it every day. The team that built it is small and the product covers a lot of ground: email, documents, tasks, chat, calls and agents. Three engineering seats are open to keep building on what shipped.

The role

A generalist seat. You take a feature from idea to shipped, front and back, anywhere in the product. Macro describes it as a seat for smart, versatile engineers who do not need an exact skill match. The language matters less than the depth and care in your work.

What you would do

  • Build across the workspace: email, documents, tasks and collaboration.
  • Own features end to end.
  • Work in TypeScript, Rust or both, whichever the work needs.
  • Sit with a small product team, in person, where decisions are quick.
  • Ship in public. Your work is on GitHub under your own name.

What they ask for

  • Real production experience as a versatile engineer. The seat is open from early career up to about twelve years in.
  • Things you built for yourself that someone can open and look at: a personal site, a code page, or writing about how you think.
  • A real interest in software as a craft.
  • No degree required. They judge the work, in conversation.
  • In New York, in the office.

Who would be successful

  • You build things for your own curiosity, and it shows online.
  • Your personal site is simple and well made.
  • You can talk through your own technical decisions and enjoy doing it.
  • Your resume says plainly what you built.
  • You have spent real time on modern tools.
  • Rust is a strong plus. So is SolidJS. Neither is required.

The tech stack

  • Engine: Rust, 42 services and 167 libraries.
  • Interface: SolidJS and TypeScript.
  • Desktop: Tauri. Mobile too.
  • Shared editing: CRDTs on Cloudflare, so documents stay in step across people and devices.
  • Licence: AGPL, fully open.

The interview process

  1. A phone call with Teo, the CTO.
  2. A visit to the New York office. It is a conversation about things you have built. No coding questions.
  3. Sometimes, a paid trial of two to five days, so both sides can see the work for real. It pays $3,000 for five days.

Salary and logistics

  • Base: $160K to $300K, depending on experience.
  • Equity: yes.
  • Where: New York, Flatiron. In the office five days a week.
  • Open seats: three.

Other open seats at Macro

  • Front-End Engineer. For someone whose strength is the interface and how it looks and feels.
  • Sales Executive. For someone who wants to put the product in front of companies.
