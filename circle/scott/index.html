How to read a Circle offer

A Staff engineer at Circle is paid in three pieces:

  • Base salary. For the Staff seat this goes up to $275,000.
  • Annual bonus. A target of 10 to 15 percent of base, paid in cash.
  • Stock, as restricted stock units. A grant of $500,000 to $800,000, vesting over four years. Circle has said this part is flexible for the right person, and grants in this seat have gone higher.

The piece most people underweight is the stock, because at most startups stock is paper. At Circle it is not. Circle is a public company listed on the New York Stock Exchange, so every piece of the grant can be sold the day it vests. Think of it as salary that arrives in a different envelope.

If the stock moves

A grant is set in dollars on the day you join, then turned into a number of shares at that day's price. From then on the shares move with the market, in both directions. Two simple cases, on the same four years:

The point is not the upside. The point is that even in the bad case the stock is real money you can sell, and the cash part of the package keeps paying either way. Most engineers also get a fresh grant each year as a refresher, which this page leaves out so the numbers stay conservative.

The stock you would leave behind

The hardest part of leaving any private company is the unvested equity. Three questions tell you what it is really worth:

  • When did the company last raise money, and at what price? A valuation that has not been refreshed in years is an old number, not a current one.
  • Is there a buyer or an exit in sight? Private shares turn into money only at a sale, a listing, or a company-run buyback. If none of those is on the calendar, the shares have no date.
  • Can you sell any of it today? If the answer is no, then every year you stay is a year of waiting, paid for with the difference in cash.

You do not have to eat that loss. It is normal, and expected at the Staff level, to ask the new company to cover the value of the unvested equity you walk away from, usually as a sign-on payment or a larger first grant. Circle has said compensation in this seat is flexible for the right person. To make that ask you need four numbers from your current company: how many options or shares you were granted, your strike price, how much is vested versus unvested, and the most recent internal share price. Bring those and the conversation becomes a straightforward negotiation.

How to look at the company

  • It is a leadership seat. Staff at Circle means setting direction for a platform other engineers build on, not carrying a ticket queue. That scope is the reason the stock piece is sized the way it is.
  • The money is on the table now, not at a future exit. Cash and sellable stock, from the first vest.
  • Nothing here is a commitment. One conversation with the team gets you the real number for your situation, including what they would do about your unvested equity. You can walk away after that with full information instead of a guess.
