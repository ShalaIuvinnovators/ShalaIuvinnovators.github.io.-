The basics, stated flat

The company

GC AI builds legal AI for in-house legal teams; the software is what a company's own lawyers rely on for drafting, reviewing, and analyzing contracts and other legal documents, with the accuracy they need to sign their names to them. Harvey sells to law firms. GC AI sells to the general counsel inside a company, which is where the name comes from. The founder built the product for the job she held for twenty years: Cecilia Ziniti was general counsel three times, including as the founding lawyer on Amazon Alexa, before Cruise and Replit. Her co-founder and chief technology officer, Bardia Pourvakil, has been building on language models since GPT-2.

Why this seat exists now

GC AI is building an Applied AI team of five engineers from an engineering group that currently numbers under 20, on the back of a $60 million Series B. The product runs four to five models tuned for different tasks, and new models are released every week, so evaluating and shipping each one is now a full team's job rather than a side task. The legal buyer pays for accuracy, so every model change is a risk to the one thing GC AI sells on. A dedicated team that tests each release before it reaches a lawyer is how the company keeps selling on it.

Why you

What you would own

Day one, an AI feature end to end: the orchestration, the retrieval, the prompt and model choice, the evaluation that proves it works, and the React surface a lawyer touches. On every model release, the evaluation and testing pass across the four or five models the product runs, and the call on whether a new one goes in. By month six, the repeatable frameworks for prototyping against a weekly model cadence, plus reviewing the pull requests the in-house lawyers submit.

The pull

A comp band that reaches $430,000 base plus equity, at a company valued at $555 million ten months ago and still growing 23 percent a month. The company's own highest-priority hire, on a team being built from the ground up, so the frameworks and the evaluation practice are yours to define. A technical co-founder who is a product engineer, not a researcher, and has been shipping on these models since GPT-2. Remote across the United States and Canada.

The money, said plainly

Up to $430,000 in base, plus equity. The band runs wide because both seats are open: strong senior engineer and a staff engineer who defines the team's practice. The bar is high for both. Remote across the United States and Canada, with three days a week in office for anyone within about fifty miles of San Mateo or Provo.

The AI feature you would point to as the one you shipped end to end, and what you would evaluate before trusting the next model release with it. That is the first real test in this seat, and it is the part I would want to hear your answer on.
