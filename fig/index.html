Most of the candidates I place are passive candidates who kept an active interest in opportunities that looked to be their next hard problem to solve. This is one of those roles. I own the search for this seat.

Company profile

A hedge fund finds mispriced risk in markets and takes the other side. FIG finds mispriced risk in the real world and takes the other side by writing the policy itself, on its own balance sheet. The founders' own comparison set is Two Sigma, Millennium and Renaissance, not other insurers. The chief executive built and sold two insurance businesses; the chief technology officer built a prediction-model platform and a systematic trading desk himself. The founders say FIG raised $107 million in seed capital.

Product

Each pod prices an underserved insurance niche with a model nobody else has, and runs its own book on the company's own balance sheet rather than someone else's capital. The data that would price these risks mostly does not exist yet, which is the edge: nobody has cleaned it, let alone modeled it. First products go live within about 90 days of the posting, five in the first year.

Tech stack

Finding and acquiring datasets nobody has assembled. Scrapers and ingestion pipelines built from nothing. Feature engineering, pricing and underwriting model design, backtesting, deployment, and live portfolio monitoring.

Ownership

One pod's risk model, from raw data to live profit and loss. Finding and acquiring datasets nobody has assembled, building pipelines and writing a scraper when nothing exists, then designing, validating, and backtesting the pricing model against the pod's strategy. Deploying it, watching model and portfolio performance, and repricing as real claims arrive.

Why them

You have built a prediction model on large, varied data you went and acquired yourself, and carried it into live use where money rode on the answer, the quant researcher's judgment and the machine learning engineer's build and ship in one person. That is the exact shape this seat is built for.
