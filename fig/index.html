Hello, this is one of those roles

I am supporting the founders at FIG on the Pod Quant / Machine Learning Engineer seat. This page is written for you, not for a list.

The company

FIG is an insurance company built to behave like a quant fund. A fund finds mispriced risk in markets and takes the other side. FIG finds mispriced risk in the real world, in things that are hard to insure, and takes the other side by writing the policy itself. It owns its own balance sheet, so it keeps the risk it writes.

Why this seat exists now

The company runs as small pods. Each one picks a market nobody serves, prices it with a model nobody else has, and runs its own book. The pricing model is the pod, so a pod does not exist without its quant. First products go live within about ninety days, five in the first year, and every one of them needs a risk model built from data that is not yet in usable form.

Why you

What you would own

One pod's risk model, from raw data to live profit and loss. Finding and acquiring the datasets nobody has assembled, building the pipelines and writing the scraper when nothing exists, then designing, validating and backtesting the pricing model against the pod's strategy. Deploying it, watching model and portfolio performance, and repricing as real claims arrive.

The pull

A book of your own, and twenty percent of its profit above a hurdle. The datasets that price a new insurance niche have often never been assembled, so the data is the edge and nobody has cleaned it yet. The chief technology officer built a systematic trading platform himself, so the pitch does not need translating.

The money, said plainly

Up to $350,000 in base, twenty percent of the pod's profit above an agreed hurdle rate, and founding equity. The client's own note is that a successful pod could put all-in comp in the seven figures. The profit share follows claims development, so it pays on insurance time rather than on a trading year. New York, hybrid. Work authorization required, no sponsorship.

The one thing I would want to talk through

How you would price a risk that has no loss history yet. That is the first problem in the seat, before any claims arrive, and it is the part I would want to hear your approach on.
