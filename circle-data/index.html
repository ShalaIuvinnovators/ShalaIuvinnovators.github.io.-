Company profile

Circle is public on the NYSE. USDC carries about 70 percent of all stablecoin volume moved in the world, and $14.8 trillion crossed its rails last quarter, up 151 percent in a year. Jeremy Allaire, the founder, built and sold Brightcove before starting Circle in 2013.

Product

USDC already moves money for regulated institutions, but it settles on chains Circle does not control, at a cost and speed those institutions cannot govern themselves. Arc is Circle's answer: its own chain, live, with BlackRock, DTCC, Visa, Mastercard, and Goldman Sachs already signed on as founding validators. The testnet alone pushed 13.6 million transactions a week before a single institution had a reason to use it beyond testing.

The opportunity

Circle has never had someone own this. Arc is about to multiply the volume running through Circle's data by an order of magnitude, and there is no standard yet for how that data gets modeled, verified, or trusted company-wide. The company said it plainly in the brief: someone who can only point to sophisticated pipelines is not the right hire. This is a leadership problem wearing an individual contributor's title, for someone who has already proven they can set a standard other teams adopt, not just build one for themselves.

Ownership

The company-wide standard for how Circle's data gets modeled, verified, and trusted, written once and used by every team after. The SLI and SLO program, with real alerting and incident response, built before Arc's volume multiplies what rides on it. The data contracts and governance rules other engineers work against, because this person set them. Day one is mapping where the current system breaks down. By month six, that map is the standard the company runs on.

Why them

You have already designed a data-quality or reliability standard that teams outside your own had to adopt, not just a pipeline you maintained alone.

Tech stack

Distributed data systems. Data modeling and pipeline architecture. SLI and SLO frameworks with real alerting. Data contracts and governance standards. CI/CD and testing standards for data. Applying AI tools inside the work itself.

The one thing I would want to talk through

How you would decide, in your first month, which team's data problem to fix first, when every team will tell you theirs is the most broken.
