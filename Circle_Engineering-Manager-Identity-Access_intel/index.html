The problem, in one picture

Send an email and it lands anywhere on earth in a second. Send a dollar and it waits for bank hours, crosses borders in days, and loses a slice to every hand it passes through. Money never got its internet.

Circle built it. USDC is a digital dollar, backed one to one by cash and short-term Treasuries, redeemable any time, live on 30 blockchains. Businesses, banks and developers plug in once and move dollars the way they move data. In 2026 Circle added the rails around it: a federal trust bank, a payments network for financial institutions, a blockchain of its own, and a toolkit for AI agents that pay for things themselves.

Why them, and not someone else

Other ways exist to put a dollar on the internet. Each stops where Circle doesn't.

Card networks and bank wires

Reach everyone, but settle in days, close on weekends and charge by the hop.

General-purpose blockchains

Open to anyone, with fees paid in a volatile token and finality in minutes.

Circle

A regulated dollar, a network where USDC itself is the gas, finality under a second, and the largest banks and payment networks running the validators.

The moat

  • Trust that took a decade. A public company, an OCC-approved national trust bank (July 2026), a New York trust company, and EURC, the leading euro stablecoin.
  • The institutions are in, not piloting. BlackRock is bringing its BUIDL fund onto Arc. DTCC plans to tokenize the assets it custodies. BNY and Standard Chartered mint and redeem USDC for their clients.
  • A network effect in motion. 175 financial institutions enrolled in the Circle Payments Network, $14.7B in annualized volume, up 76% in one quarter.
  • The agent economy settles in USDC. 99.3% of agent payments over the x402 protocol settle in USDC. Agent Stack launched in May 2026 with 900+ paid services.

Why now

Arc went live on September 16, 2026, with more than 100 applications on day one. Agent Stack is turning software agents into Circle customers. The trust bank charter opened the door to the largest institutions. Each one multiplies who signs in to Circle: developers with API keys, enterprises with single sign-on, and now agents holding scoped credentials. The Identity & Access pillar is the front door they all come through, and Circle is hiring the engineering manager who owns it.
